# Neuromarketing Web Copy – Schrijven voor het Brein

## Core Principle

People don’t read.  
They scan, feel, and decide.

Write for how the brain actually works:
fast, emotional, pattern-driven.

---

## The Brain Rules

- The brain is lazy → reduce effort
- The brain is emotional → trigger feeling first
- The brain looks for relevance → answer “what’s in it for me” instantly
- The brain scans → structure beats text length

---

## Structure First (Before Writing)

Every page should follow this order:

1. Attention
2. Relevance
3. Trust
4. Proof
5. Action

---

## 1. Attention (Stop the Scroll)

- Use a clear, benefit-driven headline
- Make it about the reader, not you
- Avoid vague claims

**Good:**
Increase your conversion without changing your traffic

**Bad:**
The future of digital excellence

---

## 2. Relevance (Why Should I Care?)

- Answer this within 3 seconds
- Focus on outcome, not features
- Mirror the problem of the reader

**Formula:**
Problem → Recognition → Desired outcome

---

## 3. Trust (Can I Believe This?)

- Use specifics (numbers, examples)
- Show real situations
- Avoid empty claims

**Examples:**
- “+32% conversion uplift”
- “Used by 120+ e-commerce teams”

---

## 4. Proof (Does It Work?)

- Case results
- Social proof
- Concrete outcomes

The brain believes what it can visualize.

---

## 5. Action (What Should I Do?)

- One clear next step
- Reduce friction
- Make it feel easy and safe

**Examples:**
- Plan your demo
- See how it works

---

## Writing Rules

- One idea per paragraph
- Max 3–4 lines per paragraph
- Use subheadings every 2–3 sections
- Write for scanning, not reading

---

## Cognitive Triggers

### 1. Loss Aversion

People fear losing more than they want gaining.

Use:
- “Stop losing revenue”
- “Don’t miss out”

### 2. Social Proof

People follow others.

Use:
- Logos
- Numbers
- Testimonials

### 3. Authority

People trust experts.

Use:
- Experience
- Data
- Recognizable brands

### 4. Simplicity

If it feels complex, people drop off.

Use:
- Clear language
- Fewer choices
- Straight paths

### 5. Curiosity

Open loops increase engagement.

Use:
- “Here’s what most teams miss”
- “The reason this works is…”

---

## Headlines That Work

- Focus on outcome
- Make it specific
- Keep it simple

**Formulas:**

- How to [result] without [pain]
- Get [result] in [timeframe]
- The smarter way to [goal]

---

## UX Writing Principles

- Buttons = action-oriented
- Labels = clear and predictable
- Forms = minimal friction

---

## Common Mistakes

- Writing from the company perspective
- Too much text without structure
- Vague benefits
- No clear next step

---

## Formula

(Relevance + Clarity + Emotion) × Simplicity = Conversion

---

## Claude Prompt

Write web copy using neuromarketing principles:

- Focus on the reader, not the company
- Make benefits immediately clear
- Use short, scannable sentences
- Trigger emotion before logic
- Reduce cognitive load
- Add proof and specificity
- Guide towards one clear action

Structure the content:
Attention → Relevance → Trust → Proof → Action

Avoid:
- vague claims
- corporate language
- long paragraphs

Always optimize for clarity and conversion.
